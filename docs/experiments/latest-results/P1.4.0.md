# P1.4.0 — Intact SimSiam under a correlated STL-10 diet

**Status: complete for the preregistered 40-pass STL-10 core study on local CPU.**

The experiment held the training examples, batch size, 1,200 optimizer updates, model initialization,
and evaluation split fixed within each replicate, then changed their order from IID to
128-image single-class blocks. Five development seeds selected that block size; ten separate seeds
(100–109) supplied the independent confirmation. The model retained its projector, predictor,
and stop-gradient. The forced-collapse configuration served only as an instrument control.

All 100 confirmation arms passed the budget and finite-metric gates. Both IID budget families
learned beyond their frozen initial B5 model, and all forced-collapse positive controls passed.

| Held-out paired contrast | Late linear-probe effect | 95% paired bootstrap interval | Projector-RankMe log ratio |
| -- | --: | --: | --: |
| IID minus stressed `accept_all` | +2.49 percentage points | +1.26 to +3.66 | +0.373 |
| Stressed `loss_half` minus `random_half` | +1.93 points | +1.02 to +2.74 | +0.091 |
| Stressed `replay_fixed` minus `accept_all` | +2.30 points | +1.01 to +3.83 | +0.323 |

Correlated ordering produced a clear projector-geometry change and a smaller positive quality
penalty. Its quality interval does **not** clear the preregistered 3-point *lower-bound* criterion
for strong degradation, although it does exclude zero. The geometry interval does clear its
registered 20% rank-ratio margin. This supports data-induced compression with associated quality
loss, but not a confirmed 3-point collapse benchmark or point-collapse interpretation.

Both interventions improved stressed quality against their **budget-matched** controls. The
two-sided paired sign-flip p values, corrected together by Holm, were 0.0078 for loss-based
admission and 0.0039 for fixed-budget replay. Their mean improvements were below the registered
3-point benefit margin, so the formal **protection verdict is false for both**. Replay's positive
development signal replicated; loss-based admission's held-out benefit was larger than its
near-zero development estimate. Neither result should be described as proof that a filter prevents
collapse. Both IID no-harm checks passed, and stressed kNN differences were positive for both
interventions.

These findings concern from-scratch SimSiam, balanced STL-10, and repeated 40-pass exposure.
Single-pass streaming, BDD transfer, warm-start preservation, and broader filtering strategies
remain untested. The 128-block diet is a selection-sensitivity pilot for Phase 2, not its certified
strong collapse-prevention baseline. Selecting a more severe block size based on these same held-out
seeds would need a new independent confirmation. Full design, seed-level effects, figures,
provenance, and the frozen protocol are
in the [P1.4.0 study record](../phase1/P1.4.0.md) and its
[compact evidence bundle](../phase1/artifacts/P1.4.0/study_summary.json).
