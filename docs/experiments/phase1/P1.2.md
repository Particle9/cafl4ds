# P1.2 — Does online adaptation beat an init-matched frozen backbone?

## Question and design

This is the first executable version of Phase 1 leg (a). For each seed, the experiment snapshots the randomly
initialized joint-embedding model before the first gradient update. The live arm then receives the correlated
four-regime stream; the B5 twin remains frozen. Both are evaluated on the same held-out examples and checkpoint grid.

The network-free first run deliberately strengthens the earlier smoke setup:

- three matched seeds (`0, 1, 2`);
- six canary classes, stronger noise and regime tint;
- a fixed synthetic dataset across seeds, while initialization and within-regime order vary;
- health evaluation every two stream updates;
- balanced, training-disjoint queries for every `(regime, canary)` cell;
- global kNN and linear probes plus a linear probe on the current and every earlier regime.

The implementation is `scripts/run_adaptation_experiment.py` with `adaptation.yaml`. Run it with:

```powershell
.\.venv\Scripts\python.exe scripts\run_adaptation_experiment.py
```

Tier-B plausibility checks.

| Global linear | −0.125, −0.063, +0.021 | **−0.056** | 1/3 |
| Final condition cells | 12 paired regime reads | **−0.031** | 3 wins, 1 tie, 8 losses |

The final condition-specific mean gains were −0.069, −0.056, −0.042, and +0.042 for regimes 0–3. Earlier-condition mean

## Verdict

**Adaptation does not beat freezing in this tested cell.** The result is internally consistent across the global and
condition-specific views: the live representation moves substantially, RankMe falls, and performance is usually below
B5. The last regime sometimes benefits, but that gain comes with losses on earlier conditiForons.

This is an exploratory synthetic, from-scratch, joint-embedding result with only three seeds. It is not the Phase-1
real-data exit decision and no significance claim is made. The next replication must run the same matched design on the
promoted BDD corpus and across the initialization sweep before halting or reframing the project.

## BDD replication protocol

`adaptation_bdd.yaml` pins the promoted BDD validation diet, the three development seeds `[11, 23, 37]`,
training-disjoint per-regime probes, and an init-matched B5 twin. Point it at the canonical BDD100K image/label layout,
then run the six-cell adaptation-intensity sweep:

On Windows, the checked launcher is the simplest route:

```powershell
.\scripts\run_p12_bdd.ps1 -BddRoot C:\data\bdd100k -Stage dev -Initialization from_scratch
```

Add `-DryRun` to validate the paths and print the command without starting training. Use `-Device cuda` when PyTorch
CUDA is installed. Pretrained and warm starts respectively require `-Initialization pretrained -Checkpoint <encoder.pt>`
and `-Initialization warm -Well <well.pt>`. The loader accepts both the legacy `labels/bdd100k_labels_images_val.json`
filename and the current Detection 2020 layout, `labels/det_20/det_val.json`; no manual rename is needed.

The equivalent direct Hydra command is:

```powershell
.\.venv\Scripts\python.exe scripts\run_adaptation_experiment.py --config-name adaptation_bdd -m `
  bdd_root=C:/absolute/path/to/bdd100k `
  optim.lr=1e-4,3e-4,1e-3 `
  update_every=1,4
```

Choose one cell using only those development seeds. Repeat the entire sweep separately for each initialization under
test:

- `init=from_scratch well=null`;
- `init=pretrained init.checkpoint=C:/absolute/path/to/encoder.pt well=null`;
- a deployment warm start with `init=from_scratch well=C:/absolute/path/to/method_well.pt`.

The update cadence is measured on the incoming-batch clock: `update_every=4` updates at steps 0, 4, 8, ... but retains
every regime boundary and the configured health-evaluation grid. The resolved learning rate and cadence are stamped into
each manifest and `comparison.json`.

Summarize a completed sweep with:

```powershell
.\.venv\Scripts\python.exe scripts\summarize_adaptation_sweep.py `
  outputs\adaptation-bdd\sweeps\<timestamp>
```

`joint_wins` is intentionally strict: within one seed, both global kNN and linear gain must be positive and final
earlier-condition gain must be non-negative. `retention_losses` counts seeds whose final earlier-condition mean is below
the frozen twin.

After selecting exactly one cell, confirm it without further tuning on five untouched seeds:

```powershell
.\.venv\Scripts\python.exe scripts\run_adaptation_experiment.py --config-name adaptation_bdd `
  bdd_root=C:/absolute/path/to/bdd100k `
  seeds=[101,103,107,109,113] seed_split=confirmation `
  optim.lr=<chosen_lr> update_every=<chosen_cadence> `
  init=<chosen_init> init.checkpoint=<checkpoint_or_null> well=<well_or_null>
```

The pre-registered decision is **Go** when adapted global accuracy and earlier-condition retention both beat B5 in at
least four of five confirmation seeds without a retention loss. A current-only gain with earlier-condition harm sends
the next experiment to replay/filtering. No B5 gain across the initialization sweep stops online representation
adaptation and reframes the project around a frozen encoder plus selection efficiency.

## Exploratory B1 reservoir replay — 2026-09-18

The first replay follow-up compares the selected B-floor cell (`lr=1e-4`, update every incoming batch, from-scratch JE)
with B1 reservoir replay on untouched development seeds `[211, 223, 227]`. B1 uses Vitter Algorithm R with capacity 256
and appends up to 32 uniformly replayed past images to each 32-image incoming batch. Run both paired arms with:

```powershell
.\.venv\Scripts\python.exe scripts\run_adaptation_experiment.py `
  --config-name adaptation_bdd_replay --multirun `
  bdd_root=C:/absolute/path/to/bdd100k device=cpu `
  filter=accept_all,reservoir
```

Artifacts: `outputs/adaptation-bdd/replay-sweeps/20260918-123318/`.

| Arm | Global kNN gain | Global linear gain | Current gain | Earlier-condition gain | Retention losses | Joint wins |
| -- | -: | -: | -: | -: | -: | -: |
| B-floor / accept all | -1.33 pp | +1.78 pp | +6.67 pp | +0.56 pp | 1/3 | 1/3 |
| B1 / reservoir replay | -1.33 pp | +5.33 pp | +6.67 pp | +2.40 pp | 1/3 | 1/3 |
| B1 / compute-matched reservoir | 0.00 pp | +1.33 pp | +6.67 pp | +2.36 pp | 0/3 | 1/3 |

B1 improved the mean linear readout and earlier-condition retention without reducing the mean current-condition gain. It
also reduced the adapted model's mean forgetting measure from 4.62 pp to 2.42 pp across these seeds. The effect was not
uniform: seed 211 became worse, while seeds 223 and 227 supplied the retention benefit. Neither arm improved mean global
kNN, and the strict joint criterion remained 1/3.

Operational B1 processes up to twice as many images per optimizer step (32 incoming + 32 replay), so replay content and
extra compute are confounded. The compute-matched control instead trains on 16 incoming + 16 replay images once warm,
while admitting all 32 arrivals to Algorithm R. Its artifacts are in
`outputs/adaptation-bdd/replay-sweeps/20260918-124430/`.

The retention effect survives compute matching: it preserves the current-condition mean, raises earlier-condition gain
relative to B-floor, and has no retention-loss seed in this small sample. Global representation gains remain weak,
however, and the strict joint criterion is still only 1/3. This is therefore a candidate for fresh paired five-seed
confirmation, not a positive result.

**Verdict: the compute-matched B1 candidate does not pass the confirmation gate.** It has only 3/5 joint wins rather
than the required 4/5 and still has one retention-loss seed. Reservoir replay is therefore a modest, heterogeneous
retention aid in this cell, not a solved anti-forgetting method. Do not tune on these confirmation seeds. The next
experiment should change the intervention or objective (for example, replay weighting/budget on new development seeds,
or a stability-aware update rule) and reserve another untouched seed set for any later confirmation.

## Exploratory B1.5 semantic deduplication plus replay — 2026-09-18

The next intervention filters near-duplicate images within each incoming batch before applying the same compute-matched
reservoir replay policy. A diagnostic-only threshold sweep on seeds `[401, 409, 419]` showed that the existing `0.9`
cosine threshold was unusable for the randomly initialized encoder: it retained only 5.76% of images. The selected
threshold, `0.998`, retained 75.53% in that diagnostic. No outcome metrics were used to choose it.

On fresh development seeds `[431, 433, 439]`, the live filter retained 76.84% of 816 arrivals on average. Artifacts:

Relative to B1 on the same seeds, B1.5 changes mean kNN by -0.80 pp, linear by -1.60 pp, current-condition gain by +4.00

test an explicit stability-aware update objective; if deduplication is revisited, its similarity signal should come from
a fixed pretrained representation or a representation-independent image metric.

## Exploratory B1-D cached-feature distillation — 2026-09-19

B1-D tests an explicit stability objective rather than another admission filter. Each image enters the same
compute-matched reservoir with its normalized backbone embedding at arrival time. On replay, the live encoder minimizes
cosine distance to that cached feature in addition to the SimSiam objective. Labels are never used for training. The
optimizer still sees 16 current and 16 replay images per step, so storage, replay, update count, and optimizer batch
size match B1.

Fresh development seeds `[467, 479, 487]` compare B1 with distillation weights `[0.1, 1.0, 10.0]`. Artifacts:
`outputs/adaptation-bdd/feature-distill-dev/20260919/`. The reusable configuration is `adaptation_bdd_distill.yaml`; its
Hydra subdirectory includes the weight to prevent sweep cells from overwriting one another.

| Arm | Global kNN gain | Global linear gain | Current gain | Earlier-condition gain | Forgetting | Retention losses | Joint wins |
| -- | -: | -: | -: | -: | -: | -: | -: |
| B1 / compute-matched reservoir | 0.00 pp | +4.00 pp | +13.33 pp | +3.57 pp | 4.77 pp | 1/3 | 1/3 |
| B1-D / feature distillation, `λ=0.1` | +0.89 pp | +4.89 pp | +6.67 pp | +3.07 pp | 3.24 pp | 1/3 | 1/3 |
| B1-D / feature distillation, `λ=1` | -1.33 pp | +4.00 pp | +6.67 pp | +2.40 pp | 5.17 pp | 1/3 | 1/3 |
| B1-D / feature distillation, `λ=10` | -1.33 pp | +2.22 pp | 0.00 pp | +1.42 pp | 5.24 pp | 1/3 | 1/3 |

The mechanism is active and dose-responsive: mean replay-feature cosine distance falls from about `0.0198` at `λ=0.1`,
to `0.0065` at `λ=1`, and `0.0016` at `λ=10`. Stronger anchoring therefore does stabilize the representation, but
excessive stability suppresses useful adaptation.

The weakest weight is the best B1-D cell. Relative to B1 it changes mean kNN and linear gains by `+0.89 pp` each and
reduces the peak-to-final forgetting measure by `1.53 pp`. However, it also reduces current-condition gain by `6.67 pp`,
reduces final earlier-condition gain by `0.50 pp`, and does not improve either retention-loss count or strict joint
wins. The other weights are worse.

**Verdict: cached-feature distillation answers its development subquestion negatively in this form.** It controls drift,
but the control is not converted into better final retention and it trades away plasticity. No cell advances to
five-seed confirmation. Do not tune these weights on confirmation seeds. A materially different target is needed before
revisiting this idea—for example, a fixed competent pretrained teacher rather than each sample's feature from an
evolving random encoder.

### Scope of the answer

This study answers one intervention question inside P1.2: **on the from-scratch BDD joint-embedding cell, does
arrival-feature distillation improve compute-matched reservoir replay?** The answer is no at the tested weights. It
corroborates the stability/plasticity trade-off but does not complete P1.3's correlation-by-horizon forgetting study.

P1.2 itself remains open at project level because its planned pretrained and warm-start initialization cells, and the
ascending few-shot readout, have not been completed. P1.1, P1.3, P1.4, P1.5, and P1.6 are also not answered by this
experiment.

### Updated scope

P1.2 now has negative evidence for both from-scratch and in-domain warm joint-embedding starts on BDD. Replay, semantic
deduplication, and arrival-feature distillation also failed their promotion criteria in the from-scratch cell. The
project-level P1.2 verdict remains open only for a genuinely independent pretrained initialization and the ascending
few-shot readout. This experiment does not answer P1.1 or the full horizon/correlation studies P1.3–P1.6.

## Independent STL-10 pretraining → BDD sweep — 2026-09-20

This run closes the independent-pretraining initialization cell for the TinyViT–SimSiam vehicle. The encoder was
pretrained without BDD data on 10,000 distinct images sampled from the official STL-10 unlabeled split, at 64 px, for 20
fixed epochs (6,220 optimizer updates). Only the encoder checkpoint was transferred; the BDD live and frozen arms
received fresh, identically initialized SimSiam projector/predictor heads. The checkpoint is
`outputs/p1.2/pretrained-stl10-unlabeled/simsiam.pt`.

The checkpoint had to beat a matched random TinyViT on held-out labeled STL-10 before it was allowed into the BDD
comparison. It passed this competence gate in both probe settings:

| -- | -: | -: | -: | -: |
| 20 support + 20 query / class | 24.0% | 25.5% | 25.0% | 29.0% |

### Updated scope

P1.2 now has negative joint-embedding evidence for all three planned initialization conditions: random from-scratch,
competent in-domain warm, and competent independent STL-10 pretraining. The remaining planned P1.2 item is the ascending

## Ascending few-shot readout — 2026-09-21

The last planned P1.2 readout reran the nominal best independent-pretraining cell unchanged: STL-10-pretrained
TinyViT–SimSiam, `lr=1e-3`, update every four batches, and development seeds `[541, 547, 557]`. After the stream, a
linear classifier was fitted on deterministic nested subsets of the fixed held-out BDD support set at 1, 2, 5, 10, 20,
and all 25 labels per scene class. The query set stayed fixed, and neither encoder was updated by this evaluation.
Artifacts: `outputs/adaptation-bdd/few-shot/20260921-stl10-pretrained/`.

| Labels per class | Mean accuracy gain | Wins | Ties | Losses |
| -: | -: | -: | -: | -: |
| 1 | +4.00 pp | 2/3 | 1/3 | 0/3 |
| 2 | -2.67 pp | 0/3 | 0/3 | 3/3 |
| 5 | +3.56 pp | 3/3 | 0/3 | 0/3 |
| 10 | +4.00 pp | 3/3 | 0/3 | 0/3 |
| 20 | -0.89 pp | 1/3 | 0/3 | 2/3 |
| 25 | +3.11 pp | 3/3 | 0/3 | 0/3 |

The curve supplies a real positive nuance: adapted features are consistently easier to separate with a linear head at 5,
10, and 25 labels per class. It is not a monotonic or label-budget-robust advantage: every seed loses at 2-shot and two
seeds lose at 20-shot. More importantly, this is an additional readout of the already non-promoted development cell; it
does not erase that cell's `1/3` strict joint wins, one retention-loss seed, or the third seed's `-4.0 pp` global kNN
result. It therefore does not trigger a post-hoc five-seed confirmation.

**Final P1.2 verdict for the tested TinyViT–SimSiam vehicle: No Go.** Ordinary online adaptation does not demonstrate a
reliable joint advantage over an exact frozen twin across initialization, representation readout, and earlier-condition
retention. The few-shot result shows localized linear separability gains rather than a uniformly better representation.
This closes P1.2's planned execution while preserving that nuance; it does not claim that all online SSL methods or
larger pretrained models must fail.

The project consequence is to stop tuning this ordinary SimSiam adaptation loop. Subsequent work should either use a
frozen competent encoder and study selection/communication efficiency, or open a separately motivated method-development
study with a materially different continual objective. P1.1 and P1.3–P1.6 remain unanswered.

## Constrained continual-adaptation follow-up — 2026-09-21

Because continual learning remains a project requirement, this follow-up tests a new stability–plasticity architecture
rather than retuning ordinary full adaptation. All arms start from the independently qualified STL-10 encoder and use
`lr=1e-3`, update every four incoming batches, accept-all selection, and untouched development seeds `[563, 569, 571]`:

- `full`: update the complete encoder and fresh SimSiam heads;
- `adapter`: freeze TinyViT, add a zero-initialized 96→16→96 residual adapter to its pooled representation, and update
    the adapter plus heads;
- `last_block`: freeze the first three transformer blocks, update only the fourth block, final normalization, and heads.

The zero-initialized adapter is an exact identity at step zero. Adapter construction restores the RNG state, so it does
not alter the paired stream/augmentation trajectory. Artifacts: `outputs/adaptation-bdd/constrained/20260921-dev/`.

| Trainable scope | Trainable / total parameters | Encoder trainable | kNN gain | Linear gain | Current gain | Earlier-condition gain | Retention losses | Joint wins |
| -- | -: | -: | -: | -: | -: | -: | -: | -: |
| Full | 464,736 / 464,736 | 324,192 | +3.56 pp | +2.67 pp | +6.67 pp | +0.69 pp | 1/3 | 2/3 |
| Adapter | 143,728 / 467,920 | 3,184 | +0.44 pp | 0.00 pp | 0.00 pp | 0.00 pp | 0/3 | 0/3 |
| Last block | 215,520 / 464,736 | 74,976 | +0.44 pp | 0.00 pp | 0.00 pp | +0.10 pp | 1/3 | 0/3 |

The constrained arms achieve stability but not useful plasticity. Adapter-only is exactly tied to the frozen model on
linear accuracy, current-condition accuracy, and earlier-condition accuracy in all three seeds; last-block adaptation is
similarly inert and introduces one small retention loss. Their few-shot curves also stay near zero (adapter gains range
`0.00–+0.89 pp`; last block `-0.44–+1.33 pp`). The SSL loss still falls because the much larger fresh
projector/predictor heads can absorb the objective while the protected representation barely moves.

Full adaptation looks positive on this particular fresh split, with two strict joint wins, but it does not reset the
earlier failed development result for the exact same cell (`1/3` joint wins on seeds `[541, 547, 557]`). Across the two
predeclared three-seed splits it has only `3/6` joint wins and two retention-loss seeds. Promoting it based only on the
favorable second split would be post-hoc seed selection.

**Verdict: neither constrained architecture advances to confirmation.** The architectural hypothesis is only
half-supported: freezing most weights does prevent drift, but with fresh trainable SimSiam heads it also starves the
representation of an effective learning signal. A material next method should pretrain and preserve the SSL heads,
freeze or tightly control them during deployment, and direct the online objective into the adapter; alternatively it
should use a fixed pretrained teacher. This result does not justify a width/LR sweep on the same seeds.

## Full-method pretraining and fixed-head adapter — 2026-09-21

This follow-up directly tests the mechanism proposed above. SimSiam was retrained with the same independent STL-10
unlabeled recipe (10,000 images, 64 px, 20 epochs, 6,220 updates), but the complete state was retained: encoder,
projector, predictor, and BatchNorm buffers. Artifacts: `outputs/p1.2/pretrained-stl10-full/simsiam.pt` (encoder) and
`outputs/p1.2/wells/simsiam_stl10_unlabeled_full.pt` (whole method). The reusable qualification entry point is
`scripts/qualify_encoder.py`.

The replacement encoder passed a matched STL-10 competence gate before BDD. At 20 support and 20 query images per class
it exactly reproduced the earlier random/pretrained results: kNN `24.0% → 25.5%`, linear `25.0% → 29.0%`. At 300 support
and 100 query images per class on the recorded train-split partition it improved kNN `24.5% → 31.3%` and linear
`31.1% → 37.6%`.

Three arms then used the complete well on untouched seeds `[577, 587, 593]`, with the prior fixed deployment setting

- `full`: update encoder and pretrained heads;

- `adapter_frozen_heads`: update only the adapter; projector/predictor parameters and BatchNorm running statistics stay
    fixed.

**Verdict: the fresh-head escape explanation is rejected as the main bottleneck.** Preserving the pretrained heads—and
even forcing the online loss through them by freezing their parameters and buffers—still gives a stable but functionally
inert adapter. No arm meets the development promotion rule, so no confirmation run is warranted. Together with the
earlier replay, dedup, distillation, last-block, and full-adaptation results, this closes ordinary SimSiam-style
adaptation for this TinyViT vehicle rather than inviting another post-hoc learning-rate sweep.

Continual learning remains central, but the next experiment should change the learning signal: for example, a fixed
competent teacher with an explicit plasticity term plus retention regularization, evaluated against the same frozen
baseline. This follow-up strengthens P1.2's No-Go conclusion; it still does not answer P1.1 or the horizon/correlation
questions P1.3–P1.6.

## Fixed-teacher plasticity + retention objective — 2026-09-21

The proposed materially different learning signal was implemented as `TeacherStudentAdapter`. TinyViT and the complete
SimSiam heads come from the qualified STL-10 well and remain fixed. A zero-initialized residual adapter is the only
trainable component. For each incoming BDD batch:

- the fixed teacher encodes the clean image and produces a fixed projector target;
- the student encodes two augmented views and its predictions are aligned to that target (plasticity);
- the student's clean-view feature is penalized for moving away from the clean teacher feature (retention).

This is label-free online learning, and the student is exactly representation-matched to its frozen baseline at step
zero. The fixed teacher, projector, predictor, and all their BatchNorm buffers remain in evaluation mode. Total
parameters are 792,112 because the fixed teacher is carried alongside the student; only the 3,184 adapter parameters
receive gradients.

The first development sweep fixed `lr=1e-3`, update cadence 4, and fresh seeds `[599, 601, 607]`, varying retention
weight `{0, 0.1, 1, 10}`. Weight zero is the plasticity-only control. Artifacts:
`outputs/adaptation-bdd/teacher-student/20260921-dev/`.

but its plasticity signal does not yield a better BDD representation. This is stronger evidence than the frozen-head
test: the loss is non-zero at initialization, a plasticity-only control was included, and a separate high-pressure check
forced measurable drift. The result closes this small-adapter TinyViT–SimSiam direction rather than motivating another
width/learning-rate sweep. It strengthens P1.2 only; the longer-horizon forgetting question P1.3 remains unanswered.

## Warm MAE existential gate — 2026-09-21

The final vehicle-level check moved P1.2 to the project's primary forgetting backbone. A locally warmed TinyViT–MAE
(`414,176` total/trainable parameters; `324,192` in the encoder) was trained on the stationary BDD validation-regime
pool until the plateau rule fired after 90 updates (`1.0403 -> 0.8792` reconstruction loss). Its reserved-probe sanity
read was 33.3% kNN and 50.0% linear accuracy. The checkpoint is therefore a competent small local well, not a claim
about a foundation-scale pretrained MAE.

The matched P1.2 drive used the same BDD validation source, held-out support/query sets, accept-all stream, and
development seeds `[11, 23, 37]` as the earlier intensity study. Each live arm and its frozen B5 twin started from the
exact same full-method checkpoint. The preregistered grid varied only learning rate `{1e-4, 3e-4, 1e-3}` and update
cadence `{1, 4}`. Artifacts: `outputs/adaptation-bdd/mae-sweeps/20260921-dev/`; reusable configuration:
`cafl4ds/configs/adaptation_bdd_mae.yaml`.

The pressure sweep exposes a stability-plasticity tradeoff rather than a hidden winning cell. Low pressure keeps drift
and RankMe close to the warm start but supplies no useful adaptation. High pressure moves the representation strongly;
at `1e-3`, cadence 1, RankMe falls sharply in two seeds and one seed gains kNN, but linear accuracy falls in two seeds

Few-shot evaluation does not rescue promotion. The largest localized gain is +5.78 pp at five shots for `1e-3`, cadence
4, but the same cell loses 10.22 pp at one shot, 4.89 pp at ten shots, 6.67 pp at 20 shots, and 3.11 pp at the full
25-shot read. This is label-budget-sensitive reorganization, not a robustly better representation. No cell advances to
the untouched five-seed confirmation stage.

**Verdict: No Go for the tested warm TinyViT–MAE vehicle.** Together with the completed TinyViT–SimSiam study, the
available small-model vehicles do not establish P1.2 leg (a): useful online adaptation over an initialization-matched
frozen encoder. This does not prove that a larger, strongly pretrained MAE or a different continual objective must fail.
It does trigger the published project stop rule for the currently scoped implementation: do not proceed to P1.3–P1.6 as
though successful local continual adaptation had been established. Reframe the next study around selection/communication
efficiency with a frozen competent encoder, or explicitly open a new higher-capacity method-development scope before
returning to the coupled continual loop.
