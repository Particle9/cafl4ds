# P1.4.0 — Intact SimSiam under correlated training: susceptibility and selection

**Status: STL-10 core study complete on local CPU; independent confirmation analyzed.**

This is the first sub-study of [P1.4](index.md), building on
[P0.2.1](../phase0/P0.2.1.md) and [P0.2.2](../phase0/P0.2.2.md). Its deliverable is a reproducible
STL-10 operating condition in which an intact SimSiam has measurable, data-induced degradation,
together with evidence about whether a small set of selection interventions changes that degradation.
An informative null is also a completed study.

## Execution record and results

The local CPU run completed all six `legacy_400` bridge arms and all 25 `matched_384`
susceptibility arms. The bridge reproduces the historical batching confound: IID uses 1,280
updates and full blocking 1,600 over 40 passes. Every matched arm instead uses 1,200 updates of
128 images. All five matched PCs reached the proposed loss/rank instrument criteria, and all five
IID models improved over their frozen initial model on the late linear probe.

| Matched ordering | Mean IID-minus-stress linear accuracy | Paired 95% bootstrap interval | Mean log projector-RankMe ratio |
| -- | --: | --: | --: |
| 128-image blocks | 3.26 points | 1.30–5.24 points | 0.409 |
| 256-image blocks | 3.88 points | 1.10–6.77 points | 0.359 |
| Full class blocks | 4.41 points | 2.43–6.54 points | 0.504 |

The preregistered development rule selected **128-image blocks**, the smallest dose clearing
both practical margins and the four-of-five directional check. These are development estimates,
not independent confirmation or evidence that a filter protects the model. The
[compact Stage-C artifacts](artifacts/P1.4.0/study_summary.json) retain seed-level effects,
provenance, trajectories, and the dose figure; bulky weights and per-step logs remain in the local
run store.

Stage D completed the two budget-matched comparisons on IID and the selected 128-block stress.
Both 128-image and 64-image IID references passed their development learning and PC checks.
Under stress, `loss_half − random_half` averaged **+0.18 accuracy points** (paired interval
−1.23 to +1.68); `replay_fixed − accept_all` averaged **+2.40 points** (paired interval
+0.90 to +4.19). Replay also moved projector geometry in the expected direction; admission
did not show a stable quality advantage over random admission. These are exploratory
development results and do not satisfy the registered 3-point protection margin. The
[frozen confirmation protocol](artifacts/P1.4.0/protocol.json) fixes the stress, budgets,
analysis, and ten independent seeds before Stage E.

Stage E completed **100 independent confirmation arms** on seeds 100–109. Every arm passed its
finite-metric and budget gate. Both IID budget families learned beyond B5, and all forced-collapse
positive controls passed the loss and projector-rank checks. Across Stages B–E, the study has
**166 real-data training arms**, plus the synthetic apparatus smoke. The confirmation analysis uses
only seeds 100–109; development seeds are not pooled into these intervals.

| Confirmatory contrast on 128-image blocks | Mean late linear-accuracy effect | Paired 95% bootstrap interval | Mean projector-RankMe log ratio | Holm-adjusted paired-test p |
| -- | --: | --: | --: | --: |
| IID `accept_all` minus stressed `accept_all` | +2.49 points | +1.26 to +3.66 | +0.373 | — |
| Stressed `loss_half` minus stressed `random_half` | +1.93 points | +1.02 to +2.74 | +0.091 | 0.0078 |
| Stressed `replay_fixed` minus stressed `accept_all` | +2.30 points | +1.01 to +3.83 | +0.323 | 0.0039 |

The diet penalty is positive on 7/10 seeds (two-sided paired sign-flip p = 0.0156). Its
projector-geometry penalty is positive on 10/10 and its bootstrap lower bound, +0.297 in log
ratio, exceeds the registered `log(1.25)` geometry margin. **The lower accuracy bound does not
exceed the registered 3-point quality margin**, so the strong geometry-associated degradation
criterion is not met. The machine-readable classification is
`geometry_change_without_confirmed_quality_harm`; here “without confirmed” means the *3-point
practical margin* was not confirmed, not that quality was unchanged. A smaller accuracy penalty is
supported by the data. The result should not be called point collapse: the PC is much more severe,
and rank change alone does not establish that fingerprint.

Both selection interventions improved stressed accuracy against their **own budget-matched
controls**, and their projector effects point in the helpful direction. Admission was positive on
9/10 seeds, replay on 10/10. The kNN differences also favored admission (+1.18 points) and replay
(+4.04 points). IID no-harm checks passed; the mean IID effects were +1.46 points for admission
and +0.13 points for replay. The stressed interventions nearly closed the mean accuracy gap to
their respective IID controls, but uncertainty around those residual gaps remains.

**Neither intervention satisfies the registered protection claim.** Each mean accuracy benefit is
below the predeclared 3-point practical margin, and the strong diet-quality-margin gate failed.
These are reproducible selection benefits in this 40-pass STL-10 setup, not a certified remedy for
a confirmed 3-point collapse/degradation condition. Admission's Stage-E benefit is stronger than
its near-zero Stage-D estimate; report that development/confirmation difference rather than
retroactively selecting a different criterion. The held-out result does not establish one-pass
transfer, BDD transfer, or preservation of an already competent warm-start model.

For Phase 2, this is a useful **selection-sensitivity pilot**, but not the preregistered strong
collapse-prevention benchmark. The 256- and 384-image block doses remain development observations;
choosing either after seeing Stage E would require a newly registered independent confirmation
block. A stronger baseline may instead come from a separate pressure axis or dataset.

## 1. Scientific question and scope

The primary question is: **does changing the ordering of otherwise identical training examples harm
an intact SimSiam, and can changing its training diet mitigate that harm at a matched budget?**

Separate three claims throughout the implementation and report:

1. **Susceptibility:** correlated training lowers downstream representation quality relative to
   matched IID training, with a corresponding projector-geometry change.
2. **Selection sensitivity:** a selection intervention changes the outcome relative to the
   appropriate budget-matched control. The change may help or hurt.
3. **Protection:** an intervention improves downstream quality under stress, with corroborating
   health evidence, while preserving useful learning. This is stronger than selection sensitivity.

The core model is intact: predictor on, stop-gradient on, `collapse_alpha=0`, `stopgrad_beta=1`.
The ablated SimSiam is an instrument positive control (PC), never the model on which filter efficacy
is claimed. Labels construct the synthetic ordering and evaluate representations; they never score
or steer the deployed selection policies.

P1.4.0 covers the **STL-10 mechanism pilot** and a conditional single-pass check. It does not close
Phase 1's BDD exit gate. BDD transfer remains subsequent P1.4 work, systematic capacity/init sweeps
remain P1.5, a broad filter comparison remains Phase 2, and health-driven policy adaptation remains
Phase 3. No controller is introduced here.

The primary from-scratch experiment measures a failure to learn equally useful features. It must
not be described as erosion of an already competent representation. A warm-start extension is
specified separately below if preservation of established competence becomes the next question.

## 2. Evidence inherited from Phase 0

P0.2.2 established instrument behavior under forced collapse. It did not establish that the intact
correlated arm suffers downstream harm or that filtering repairs it. In particular, a smaller
healthy/PC separation is not itself a measure of diet-induced degradation.

Re-reading the four-seed E3 artifacts gives these intact-arm means:

| Ordering | Backbone RankMe | Projector RankMe |
| -- | --: | --: |
| IID | 5.13 | 29.71 |
| Blocks of 128 | 3.41 | 15.67 |
| Blocks of 256 | 3.45 | 16.91 |
| Full class blocks | 3.08 | 15.09 |

Source: [P0.2.2 E3 artifacts](../phase0/artifacts/P0.2.2/E3/). These runs had probes disabled.
They motivate an ordering experiment; they do not establish harmful collapse, a monotonic dose
response, or an optimal block size. The later 16-seed calibration also cautions against interpreting
small differences among correlated conditions as a reliable intermediate-block effect.

Carry forward four measurement constraints:

- Read collapse geometry primarily at the **projector**. Backbone RankMe remains a reference.
- Healthy training can have an early RankMe dip. Random-init rank is not a healthy-quality target.
- Keep the evaluation examples and class coverage fixed. P0.2.4's low-diversity false alarms make
  changing the evaluation population an unacceptable confound.
- The correlated single-pass detector was marginal. Do not reuse a Phase-0 ratio of 2.0 as an
  absolute collapse threshold or silently transfer it to another recipe.

## 3. Experimental recipe and the batching correction

Use the P0.2.1 recipe as the starting point, pinned explicitly in a dedicated study config:

| Component | Primary value |
| -- | -- |
| Data | STL-10 labelled train split; all ten classes, labels hidden from training/selection |
| Input | 32 × 32, existing augmentation recipe unchanged |
| Encoder | Tiny ViT, embedding 96, depth 4, three attention heads, patch size 8 |
| SimSiam heads | `proj_hidden=256`, **`proj_dim=128`**, `pred_hidden=128` |
| Objective | Intact SimSiam; no partial-collapse knobs |
| Optimizer | AdamW, LR `1e-3`, weight decay `0.05`, gradient clip `1.0` |
| Schedule | Warmup/cosine, warmup fraction `0.1`, minimum LR fraction `0` |
| Incoming batch | 128 images |
| Primary horizon | 40 passes, fixed ordering reused each pass as in the existing loop |
| Held-out reservations | Per class: 40 support, 50 query, 10 additional per-era examples |
| Primary training population | **384 images per class**, the same chosen IDs in every ordering |

The distinction between projector hidden width and output width matters: the current SSL config
sets the output to 128. Record the resolved config rather than inferring dimensions from prose.

**Why 384 rather than the historical 400?** `EraStream` flushes a batch at every class/era boundary.
With 400 training examples per class and batch size 128, IID produces 32 updates per pass, while
full class blocking produces 40, including ten 16-image batches. The historical comparison thus
changes batch-size distribution, update count, and LR schedule length along with ordering.

With 384 per class, every primary ordering produces exactly 30 batches of 128 per pass: 3,840 raw
examples, 1,200 updates over 40 passes. Blocks of 128, 256, and full blocks of 384 have only full
batches. The 256 condition ends with a 128-image remainder per class, which is valid and must be
documented. This removes 4% of the historical training population, so **re-certify the IID healthy
reference and PC under this recipe** rather than assuming its health transfers.

Preserve a separate `legacy_400` profile for historical reproduction. Never pool its measurements
with the `matched_384` causal comparison. Do not change `EraStream`'s default batch semantics just
to make the study work. Setting `drop_last=true` alone is not a solution: current era-boundary
partial batches are still emitted.

The core dose sweep is IID, 128, 256, and full 384-image blocks. All correlated batches remain
single-class; the doses vary **how long training stays on that class**, not within-batch class
diversity. Do not add block sizes below 128 under the current iterator: they would shrink batches.
A mixed-batch/partial-blocking study would need an explicit batching extension and its own controls.

## 4. Data identity, initialization, and reference arms

Build an immutable split manifest before ordering. It contains source identity, sample IDs, the
support/query/per-era/train partitions, seed components, and hashes. Every arm for a replicate
uses those exact partitions. Preserve labels only in the construction/evaluation sidecar.

Separate `split_seed`, `init_seed`, `order_seed`, `train_seed`, `selection_seed`, and `eval_seed`,
derived reproducibly from a replicate ID. Fix splits before varying class order: currently changing
`class_order` also changes the order in which the stream consumes splitting RNG. Across replicates,
use reproducible class permutations so the same semantic class is not always last; within a
replicate, share the permutation across all correlated doses and policies. Reuse that order across
passes to retain the Phase-0 protocol.

Construct one initial full SimSiam state per replicate and clone it for all intact arms. This
includes encoder, projector, predictor, and BatchNorm buffers. Each arm has its own optimizer,
scheduler, monitor, and selector. Do not accidentally share mutable objects between arms.

Keep these references distinct:

| Reference | Purpose |
| -- | -- |
| Intact IID trajectory, matched to each training-budget family | Healthy learning trajectory and causal comparator for the diet |
| Frozen final IID model from that replicate | Fixed healthy geometry/quality anchor after certification |
| B5: frozen initial full model | Measures the value of adapting; random rank alone does not establish competence |
| Forced-collapse PC on IID | Verifies that the relevant measurement family still detects induced collapse |

The frozen IID anchor is measured on the same held-out set as the live arms. Its endpoint is not
used to label ordinary early-training underdevelopment as collapse; use the matched IID trajectory
and late-window criteria below. A PC on the selected stressed diet is an optional specificity
diagnostic if the live read becomes ambiguous, not a replacement for the intact benchmark.

Make apparatus certification explicit for each budget family. Require valid manifests/budgets,
finite reference runs, a PC loss floor at or below `-0.9`, and late-window IID/PC projector RankMe
separation of at least 2.0 in every calibration replicate. The last two values are proposed
Phase-0-derived **instrument checks**, not thresholds for the intact stressed arm. Also require
useful IID learning: a mean linear-probe improvement over B5 of at least 3 points during development,
positive on at least 4/5 seeds; on confirmation report its paired interval and require its lower
bound to exceed zero. Inspect the late IID trajectory for unresolved transients before freezing it
as a reference. Keep calibration failures in the record and invalidate the affected comparison
family rather than silently excluding seeds. If the 384-image recipe fails these checks, resolve
or report the reference problem before launching its policy matrix.

## 5. Minimal intervention matrix

Use three intervention comparisons. Their budgets answer different questions, so report them
separately rather than declaring an overall winner from incomparable runs.

| Arm | Images used per update | Memory | Valid comparison / question |
| -- | -- | -- | -- |
| `accept_all` | All 128 current images | None | IID vs stressed: does the diet cause harm? |
| `random_half` | Uniform 64 of the 128 current images | None | Controls reduced exposure and smaller batches |
| `loss_half` | Highest-loss 64 of the same incoming 128 | None | vs `random_half`: does content-dependent admission matter? |
| `replay_fixed` | 64 random current + 64 past images when history permits | Reservoir capacity 256 | vs `accept_all`: does replacing part of a fixed training budget with history help? |

Run all four on both IID and the selected stress condition for confirmation. This supplies healthy
no-harm controls as well as stressed comparisons. Use the existing loss-gate criterion with the
scoring safeguards below; do not tune its fraction against confirmation results.

The pure-admission pair has 1,200 updates of 64 images; the full-budget pair has 1,200 updates of
128. Both families observe the same 153,600 raw image arrivals over 40 passes. Report trained image
presentations separately: 76,800 and 153,600 respectively, plus selection-forward costs. This is not
a claim of matched wall-clock/FLOPs between random selection and loss scoring.

For `replay_fixed`, draw only from history available **before** the current batch, then ingest all
128 arriving images into the reservoir. Use `r=min(64, history_available)` replay images and
`128-r` uniformly selected current images, keeping training batch size exactly 128 even during
startup. Keep the buffer across passes and explicitly report that it samples arrival events;
multi-epoch arrivals may refer to an image seen before. Track unique IDs and event IDs separately.

This fixed-budget replay adapter is a deliberate new experimental mode. Today's `ReservoirReplay`
appends replay to all admitted images and stores only admitted inputs. Its default would enlarge
the batch and confound the comparison. Preserve that existing behavior; do not silently reinterpret
the stock `reservoir` config. The replay arm tests use of memory, not the superiority of a learned
admission criterion. It can pass even if `loss_half` has no advantage over random admission.

Run an IID `random_half` healthy reference and corresponding forced-collapse `random_half` PC to
check the 64-image training family separately. If its healthy control fails, the admission pair is
not an interpretable protection test; report that limitation without calling the loss filter a
failure. The 128-image ordering/replay experiment can remain valid.

**Dedup is a conditional follow-up, not part of the primary matrix.** Its variable acceptance
requires a random-count-matched control: record each dedup decision's kept count, then replay those
counts on the same incoming stream with uniform random IDs. Match skipped updates and LR schedules
as well. Both arms must use the same scoring mode and audit conventions. A 0.9 cosine threshold
must not be assumed to remove a useful amount of data at random initialization.

A rejection-only filter cannot introduce an absent class into a homogeneous batch. It can reduce
exposure or select different examples within that class. The replay arm supplies the distinct
cross-time diversity mechanism. This limitation should shape interpretation of an admission null.

## 6. Make selection and measurement observational

`SimSiam.per_sample_loss()` uses `no_grad`, but draws two stochastic views and calls heads with
BatchNorm. With the model in training mode, scoring can update BN statistics and consume the
training RNG even though it has no gradients. Uncontrolled, the comparison would change the model
before selecting any examples.

Add a scoped, read-only scoring context for this study:

- Save and restore module training modes; score in eval mode so BatchNorm buffers and dropout do
  not introduce policy-specific state updates. This means selection uses eval-mode loss, which must
  be explicit in the config and report.
- Use a selection RNG stream keyed by replicate and arrival step. Save/restore the training RNG
  on CPU and the active accelerator, not only CPU. Verify the backend-specific implementation.
- Preserve the model's parameters and buffers exactly during scoring. The next training call
  restores training mode and draws its own augmentation views.
- Give uniform admission and reservoir sampling their own generators. Keep evaluation RNG isolated
  too. Two equal-budget arms may share training RNG sequences without seeing the same selected
  images; do not claim identical per-image augmentations after the policies diverge.

Re-run the relevant P1.0.1 identity checks on this actual pathway. Its prior certificate covered
same-seed reconstruction under the old execution pattern; it is not a blanket certificate for
filters with extra stochastic forwards or variable batch sizes.

Instrument selection provenance without changing the existing label-free `StreamBatch` contract:
use traced study adapters exposing selected input-row indices, replay event IDs, counts, and scores
to a recorder. Associate those indices with the manifest in the recorder. Labels/class counts are
joined offline for analysis and never returned to `FilterContext`.
The inherited `StreamBatch.era` field correlates with class in this synthetic dataset: policies
must explicitly ignore it as well. It is logging metadata, not a permitted class-balancing signal.

## 7. Measurement protocol

Measure a true **pre-update** state at `optimizer_step=0`. The current loop's first health record
at `step=0` follows an update, so it must not be relabelled as pristine initialization. Add opt-in
initial measurement and explicit clocks without changing historical logs by default.

Log `arrival_step`, `optimizer_step`, `epoch`, raw examples seen, selected current examples,
replayed examples, trained presentations, realized batch size, LR, loss, pre-clip gradient norm,
and skipped-update counts. Use integer sample/update clocks for joins. In the primary matrix every
arrival produces one update, so schedules are identical within each budget family. An unexpected
skip or short batch is a protocol violation, not an invisible reduction in training.

Evaluate geometry on the same 500-image, ten-class query set at initialization, every epoch end,
and the final state. Use explicit epoch-end checkpoints rather than the present modulo convention
that reads just after the first update of an epoch. For a conditional single-pass run, use five
equally spaced update checkpoints plus the initial/final state.

| Measurement | Role |
| -- | -- |
| `rankme_proj` | Primary collapse-geometry endpoint |
| `mean_feature_var_proj` | Supports a point-collapse interpretation; noisy, never sufficient alone |
| `offdiag_cov_proj` | Reports redundancy structure; not required to fire for point collapse |
| `uniformity_proj`, `alignment_proj` | Supporting geometry; alignment alone cannot certify health |
| Backbone RankMe and drift | Context and diagnostics, not standalone collapse verdicts |
| Backbone linear probe accuracy | Primary downstream outcome, labels evaluation-only |
| Backbone kNN accuracy, `k=20` | Independent probe-method corroboration; not statistically independent data |
| Per-class accuracy from a shared ten-way classifier | Checks whether a global mean hides losses concentrated in earlier classes |

Keep probe support at 400 fixed images and query at 500, with feature standardization fit on
support only. Retain the existing probe settings, record convergence and solver details, and do
not tune regularization on the query labels. Run kNN every epoch; run the linear probe at epochs
0, 5, 10, 20, 30, and 36–40. Cache detached features at those checkpoints so probe fitting and
per-class analysis can be rerun without retraining the encoder. Refresh the probe on frozen
features at each checkpoint; this evaluates feature utility, not a frozen classifier's frame drift.
The current linear probe uses `C=1.0`, `max_iter=1000`, and support-fitted standardization; pin those
values and the library version. For the single-pass extension, both probes run at all declared
checkpoints; its endpoint is the final state, not an unavailable last-five-epoch average.

Do not use the current `PerEraProbe` BWT matrix as the primary multi-epoch endpoint. It keys rows
by era ID, so recurring class blocks overwrite rows and the numeric era ordering does not describe
chronological exposure. A ten-way probe with checkpoint-indexed per-class results answers this
study's question without a new continual-task metric. Genuine BWT needs a separate task protocol.

Use both a frozen IID endpoint and the matched IID trajectory to interpret movement. Report the
last-five-epoch mean as the primary endpoint and the final checkpoint as a secondary endpoint.
Persist full trajectories; a final rebound or an early benign dip must remain visible.

If projector geometry changes while backbone probes hold, investigate measurement or BN-state
effects before naming collapse. An optional diagnostic can recalibrate BN on a **clone** using a
fixed training-only reference set, then remeasure; retain the original measurement as primary and
never adapt BN on query/support evaluation images. Record this as a diagnostic intervention.

## 8. Effect definitions, thresholds, and statistical decisions

For each seed and budget-matched pair, let `Q` be late-window linear accuracy and `R` be late-window
projector RankMe. Compute seed-level paired effects:

- Diet quality penalty: `D_Q = Q_IID - Q_stress`.
- Diet geometry penalty: `D_R = log(R_IID / R_stress)`.
- Admission benefit: `B_Q = Q_loss_half - Q_random_half` on the same diet.
- Replay benefit: `B_Q = Q_replay_fixed - Q_accept_all` on the same diet.
- Stress specificity: benefit on stressed data minus benefit on IID data, within each family.

A positive quality penalty means worse features under stress; a positive benefit means better
features under the intervention. Report geometry effects alongside quality effects in the same
orientation. Stress specificity is useful context, not a requirement that a useful filter must
have zero benefit on IID. A filter can also move health in the harmful direction.

**Proposed practical margins, to be frozen before confirmation:** 3 percentage points in linear
accuracy and a 20% reduction in projector RankMe (`D_R >= log(1.25)`). These are study design
margins, **not Phase-0-calibrated definitions of collapse**. They deliberately allow an intermediate
effect rather than requiring a forced-collapse-sized 2× separation. Development data may justify
a documented revision before registration; confirmation data may not.

Use paired seed-level estimates and 95% confidence intervals, with a predeclared paired bootstrap
over replicate IDs (10,000 resamples, fixed analysis seed). Report every seed, mean, median, range,
and sign consistency. Checkpoints and query images are not independent training replicates.
Repeated healthy/healthy comparisons diagnose noise but their overlapping pairs do not create
extra independent seeds. With only ten confirmation seeds, intervals remain approximate; describe
borderline effects as inconclusive rather than proving absence.

For a strong confirmatory diet-degradation claim, require the lower confidence bound for `D_Q`
to exceed the registered quality margin. For a strong geometry-supported claim, require the lower
bound for `D_R` to exceed its registered margin too. Report estimates that exceed a margin while
their intervals do not as suggestive. Separate continuous evidence from these binary summaries.

Choose one primary stress condition before confirmation. Treat the admission and replay quality
contrasts as a two-test confirmatory family; use Holm correction on paired tests if significance
labels are reported, and show unadjusted effect intervals explicitly as such. Geometry and kNN
corroboration do not license selecting a more favorable primary endpoint after inspection.
Specify the paired tests as two-sided sign-flip tests of the seed-level mean effect, enumerating
all 1,024 sign assignments at ten seeds; this uses an exchangeable/symmetric paired-null
assumption, which must be stated. Use the signed effect to distinguish benefit from harm.

For a protection claim, require a mean stressed quality benefit of at least 3 points with a lower
95% interval bound above zero and a Holm-adjusted paired-test result below 0.05, corroborating
geometry in the expected direction, and an IID no-harm check whose upper confidence bound on the intervention's
quality loss is below 3 points. Demonstrate useful adaptation against B5. A policy that merely
stops learning or reduces harmful exposure to the frozen floor is reported as a retention/learning
tradeoff, not successful protection of useful adaptation. Partial and complete rescue are distinct:
complete rescue additionally puts stressed quality within the registered margin of its IID
reference. Do not require damage to be permanent for prevention to matter.

Rank/quality co-degradation warrants **geometry-associated degradation** or **evidence consistent
with partial collapse**. Reserve point-collapse language for a supporting near-constant/low-variance
fingerprint relative to the measured PC. A rank drop alone is never a point-collapse verdict.

## 9. Staged execution and bounded run budget

Implement a manifest-driven runner so each seed/arm is resumable independently. Stages are
sequential scientific decisions; separate arms may run concurrently on available hardware without
changing seed assignment or sharing state. Time one complete real-data arm before estimating
wall-clock cost. No accelerator-time estimate is assumed in this plan.

| Stage | Runs and output | Advancement rule |
| -- | -- | -- |
| A — apparatus | Network-free synthetic smoke; identity, scoring, clocks, artifacts and all selectors | Engineering assertions pass; no synthetic scientific claims |
| B — historical bridge | `legacy_400`: IID intact, full-block intact, IID PC, seeds 0–1; 6 training runs | Establish provenance and explain changes; this small bridge is not a new statistical claim |
| C — matched susceptibility | `matched_384`: IID intact + three correlated doses + IID PC, seeds 0–4; 25 training runs; B5 measured only | Healthy baseline and PC are valid; inspect unfiltered diet effects |
| D — selection development | At IID and selected stress: `random_half`, `loss_half`, `replay_fixed`, seeds 0–4; 30 runs, plus 5 IID 64-image PCs | Validate both budget families and freeze all analysis/policy choices |
| E — independent confirmation | Seeds 100–109: both diets × four primary arms, plus IID PCs for both budget families; 100 training runs | Apply locked protocol, retain all seeds, report outcomes even on a null |

Maximum core budget if all stages proceed: **166 training runs**, plus measured-only references and
cheap synthetic checks. Identical IID/reference runs are cached by full provenance rather than
recomputed. At the matched horizon, each trained arm has 1,200 updates. Stages D/E are conditional;
failure to find a useful condition in C does not authorize an unbounded search.

Choose the stress condition using **unfiltered** Stage-C results, before any policy results: prefer
the smallest of 128, 256, full blocks showing quality and geometry penalties at the proposed
practical margins, with the same direction in at least 4/5 seeds. Do not assume the curve is
monotonic. If no condition qualifies, report that outcome and the entire dose map. A condition with
quality loss but no geometry signal can support a separately labelled degradation study, but
cannot silently become the promised collapse benchmark.

Freeze the chosen condition, margins, primary endpoints, selection fraction, memory size, scoring
mode, seeds, and multiplicity handling in `protocol.json` before Stage E. Hash it into each run.
The development seeds are not pooled into confirmation estimates. The seed blocks are independent
training/split replicates from the same dataset, not evidence of generalization to a new dataset.
Infrastructure failures may be rerun identically and logged. Algorithmic divergence and poor
seeds remain outcomes; do not replace them with more favorable seeds.

If Stage E refutes the unfiltered susceptibility result, report non-replication. Do not select a
new stress condition on those same confirmation seeds and relabel it confirmatory.

### Conditional extensions with explicit limits

- **Single-pass transfer:** after the core result, run the same two diets and four policies for
  one pass on five new seeds: 40 training runs. Use a separately declared 30-update schedule and
  no full-rank expectation inherited from a mature model. Keep long-horizon PC/healthy references
  as diagnostics, never zip their epochs to this stream. A null can mean insufficient learning or
  insufficient detection; report both. This checks persistence of the phenomenon, not BDD validity.
- **Batch-size sensitivity:** if the ordering effect is positive, compare IID/stress at batch 64
  on five development replicates before broadening claims. Both diets retain the same image IDs,
  horizon, and within-comparison schedule. The changed total updates mean this is sensitivity to
  the batch/optimization recipe, not a perfectly isolated mechanism test. Wide capacity sweeps
  belong to P1.5.
- **Dedup:** only after variable-count accounting is implemented, with the count-matched random
  control described above. Treat it as exploratory unless a new confirmation block is registered.
- **Warm-start preservation:** branch from a certified full SimSiam checkpoint, including heads
  and BN buffers. Copy optimizer state across adapting arms and declare an identical continuation
  schedule without an accidental LR restart. Include IID continuation and frozen-checkpoint B5.
  Fresh heads would confound the question. This is a new init protocol, not a reinterpretation of
  the from-scratch results.

Neither class imbalance nor heavier augmentation is added after a null within the same claimed
ordering experiment. They are separate pressure axes requiring a new registered extension. This
keeps a valid null from becoming a search for any condition where a favored filter wins.

## 12. Verification and acceptance

Write tests for the invariants that determine whether the experiment answers its question:

1. **Data:** identical training IDs and held-out sets across orderings; disjoint partitions; class
   permutation leaves partitions unchanged; exactly thirty 128-image batches per matched pass.
2. **Isolation:** cloned initial full states are identical; running one arm cannot mutate another;
   scoring and monitoring preserve model parameters, BN buffers, modes, and training RNG.
3. **Selection:** random admission returns the exact quota without duplicates; replay uses only
   prior arrivals, honors capacity, has a fixed update size, and has correct startup behavior;
   traced IDs reproduce the actual tensor selection. Offline labels never reach selector inputs.
4. **Clocks:** pre-update measurement is truly initial; epoch-end readings occur after the correct
   update; LR sequences and counts match within budget families; skips remain visible and invalidate
   a supposedly fixed-budget run. Empty/sub-minimum batches do not silently pass.
5. **Evaluation:** probes train on support only; the fixed query set is shared across arms; missing
   or non-converged probes are flagged; recurring eras do not overwrite checkpoint-indexed results.
6. **Analysis:** known paired examples give the expected effect signs, margins, interval behavior,
   and outcome categories. A geometry-only change, quality-only change, null, failed PC, divergence,
   and beneficial/harmful policy effect remain distinct. Reject mixed protocols or reused test seeds.
7. **Artifacts:** strict JSON round trip, complete reference resolution, no duplicate-role loss,
   resumability/cache validation, and preservation of legacy default behavior.

Run the affected stream/filter/loop/monitor/harness/analysis unit tests and the repository's lint
and type checks after implementation. Run the complete synthetic study pipeline before real data.
Check actual data/config/budget manifests on the first real-data arm before launching the remaining
matrix. Re-certify CPU/accelerator behavior qualitatively and document numerical tolerance;
cross-device bit identity is not assumed. Scientific effect sizes are never unit-test assertions.

## 13. Interpretation and completion

| Observation | Conclusion and next action |
| -- | -- |
| Healthy reference does not learn, PC fails, or budgets differ unexpectedly | Apparatus/recipe unresolved; fix or document before interpreting filter efficacy |
| Geometry falls but probes hold | Compression or a measurement effect remains plausible; not a confirmed harmful-collapse benchmark |
| Probes fall but collapse geometry holds | Data-induced quality degradation exists; label it accordingly and investigate mechanism |
| Geometry and probes fall; matched filter improves both | Candidate protectable degradation condition, with strength set by confirmation and controls |
| Replay helps but loss selection does not beat random | History is useful in this regime; informative admission has not earned an advantage |
| Random admission helps as much as loss selection | Reduced exposure/batch recipe is sufficient; no evidence of content-selection benefit |
| A filter harms health at matched budget | Selection sensitivity exists, but this policy is not protective |
| Confirmation is null or too uncertain | Bound the tested envelope and report precision; do not claim all intact SimSiam regimes are robust |
| Effect holds at 40 passes but not one pass | Multi-epoch mechanism only; streaming transfer remains unresolved |

P1.4.0 is complete when the apparatus and controls are verified, the registered experiment has a
reproducible outcome (positive, negative, or inconclusive), the per-seed evidence and analysis are
available, and the report states exactly which claims transfer. A successful baseline handoff to
Phase 2 includes the pinned intact recipe, the stress condition, matched controls, usable readers,
quality effects, uncertainty, and known limits. It never promotes an architecture-ablation rescue
or a rank-only movement into a filtering-protection claim.

On implementation, register P1.4.0 as the first concrete sub-study in the Phase-1 index while
retaining the remaining P1.4 BDD scope explicitly. Publish `latest-results/P1.4.0.md` only when there
are actual findings. This planning document alone does not mark P1.4 or any Phase-1 gate complete.
