# M6.2.0 — routing-dependent expert learning rates

Repeat M6.1.0 with a separate AdamW learning rate for each expert in each MoE layer. Apply the same intervention to both
the learned and fixed-GMM routing arms. The purpose is to reduce updates to experts receiving little probability mass
after AdamW's moment normalization, and test whether this improves reconstruction, retention or representation health.

For the actual training minibatch, including learned-gate noise, compute

\[
q_{\ell,e}=\frac{1}{B}\sum_i p_{\ell,i,e},\qquad
s_{\ell,e}=\min(1,4q_{\ell,e}),\qquad
\eta_{\ell,e}=10^{-3}s_{\ell,e}.
\]

The probability readout is detached and the rate is set immediately before `AdamW.step()`. Uniform routing preserves the
original rate. A 1% expert mass gives a rate of 0.00004; 60% mass is capped at 0.001. Use each minibatch directly,
without a moving average. Shared encoder/attention/decoder parameters and learned gates retain 0.001. The forward
mixture, loss, gradients and global gradient clipping remain as in M6.1.0.

AdamW first and second moments and step counters advance normally for every expert, including when its rate is zero.
There are no momentum resets, skipped expert steps or special handling of inactive gradients. Weight decay keeps its
coefficient of 0.05; its parameter update scales with the expert learning rate as part of ordinary AdamW behavior.

## Matched protocol

All other settings follow [M6.1.0](M6.1.0-image-routing-mae.md): the existing 61,555-image BDD train cache, unchanged
training and evaluation panels, 18 time×weather regimes in each of two passes, batch 64, five seeds 0–4, and 1,904
updates per arm. The small from-scratch MAE uses image-level top-4 mixtures of four experts in encoder layers 2/3. All
four experts execute. Original M6.0.0 all-data GMM probabilities are reused without refitting. Runtime remains two CPU
workers with four threads each. Arm execution order alternates across seeds.

Before training, the runner checks the saved M6.1.0 configuration, dataset and conditioning fingerprints, evaluation
panels, runtime versions, and model/evaluation/data-order source hashes. After training, comparisons also require
identical initialization hashes and every training image ID/order, augmentation hash and mask-noise hash across
experiments, pairing the same router and seed. The M6.1.0 outputs are read without modifying them.

The original health signals, fixed-panel per-regime reconstruction trajectories, block-exit deterioration measures,
minibatch top-1 routing summaries and expert gradient norms are retained. Each step additionally records
`l{layer}_expert_lr_scale`, `l{layer}_expert_lr`, `shared_and_router_lr`, and the actual post-AdamW expert
parameter-step L2 norm `l{layer}_expert_update_norm`, including weight decay. Per-step rates are audited against the
recorded mean probabilities. Final optimizer states are checked for normally advancing expert counters.

The rate scaling changes parameter movement as intended. Across five seeds, after the first 476 updates, GMM layer-2
experts 0/1 have mean distances from initialization of **0.810/0.531**, versus **4.292/4.061** with constant rates.
These are the low-mass experts during the initial daytime blocks. Active experts 2/3 move **2.273/2.056**, versus
**2.234/2.014** previously. These are cumulative distances, not sums of update norms. Full seed/layer/expert milestone
records are in `expert_displacement.json` and the notebook.

This stronger protection does not establish better final performance or retention in the present five-seed experiment.
For GMM, final MSE changes by −0.000614 and pass-2 deterioration by +0.000266 relative to constant rates; both paired
intervals include zero. GMM's first-pass time-mean MSE has a worse point estimate (+0.006889), also with an interval
including zero. Thus the parameter-protection mechanism works, but preservation of the early performance advantage is
not demonstrated by these results. Shared attention, encoder and decoder parameters continue training in every block.

Within M6.2.0, mean endpoint MSE is **0.719137 learned / 0.718254 GMM**. The paired GMM-minus-learned interval is
[−0.007612, +0.005845]. Pass-2 deterioration is **0.012960 / 0.008702**, with a GMM-minus-learned interval of
[−0.008873, +0.000357]. These remain descriptive results under the small architecture, two passes and fixed panels; some
rare regimes have very few validation images. Neither comparison establishes a clear routing advantage.

The HTML export and default/alternate notebook controls were validated. The 62-test focused suite, follow-up analysis
checks, pinned pre-commit checks and dependency audit passed.

## Additional GMM arm: uncapped 4q

Add a third arm with **GMM routing and `s_e = 4*q_e`**, retaining every other training setting. Expert rates can reach
0.004, while shared parameters retain 0.001. The four expert rates sum to approximately 0.004 per layer on each
minibatch; the capped GMM rule can have a lower total. The optimizer still advances all moments and counters normally;
there are no momentum-related interventions. AdamW's weight-decay update uses each expert's applied learning rate.

This arm uses the same five seeds and 1,904 updates/seed. Its initial tensors, training IDs/order, augmented views,
masks, GMM probability vectors, top-1 readouts and fixed evaluation routing panels are paired against the completed
capped GMM arm. The original learned/capped and GMM/capped training artifacts remain unchanged. New checkpoints and
traces are stored separately in `outputs/m6_2_0_gmm_uncapped/` with their own frozen protocol and source snapshots.

### Uncapped-arm results — 2026-11-04

All five added runs completed 1,904 updates each (9,520 total updates, 609,280 image exposures). All 19,040 layer-level
rate vectors match uncapped `4*q_e`, shared rates stayed fixed, and every expert's AdamW counter advanced to 1,904.
Initial tensors, image order, augmentations, masks, fixed routing readouts and evaluation routing panels match the
capped GMM arm. The independent audit verifies the original GMM table and preserves all 86 snapshotted capped scientific
artifacts byte-for-byte.

| Outcome | Learned capped | GMM capped | GMM uncapped | Uncapped − capped GMM, paired descriptive 95% interval |
| -- | -- | -- | -- | -- |
| Final natural MSE | 0.719137 | 0.718254 | 0.741105 | +0.022851 [+0.003957, +0.041746] |
| Pass 1 time-mean MSE | 0.865206 | 0.863165 | 0.875859 | +0.012694 [-0.000948, +0.026336] |
| Pass 1 endpoint-minus-exit MSE | -0.005987 | -0.010673 | -0.003219 | +0.007454 [+0.002490, +0.012418] |
| Pass 2 endpoint-minus-exit MSE | 0.012960 | 0.008702 | 0.002952 | -0.005750 [-0.012910, +0.001409] |
| Final RankMe | 16.821412 | 16.872371 | 13.543133 | -3.329238 [-5.462960, -1.195515] |
| Balanced linear-probe accuracy | 0.391000 | 0.360000 | 0.423000 | +0.063000 [+0.022384, +0.103616] |

Uncapping raises final reconstruction MSE by **3.18%**, with worse MSE in every seed. Final RankMe also decreases in
every seed. Balanced linear-probe accuracy improves in every seed, by **6.3 percentage points** on average. The paired
intervals exclude zero for these three contrasts. These are descriptive comparisons across five seeds and the existing
fixed panels, with multiple health outcomes measured; the results support a performance tradeoff rather than a uniform
improvement or deterioration.

Pass-2 endpoint-minus-exit MSE has a smaller point estimate for uncapped GMM in all five seeds, but its paired interval
includes zero. This should be read alongside its higher absolute reconstruction error. The pass-1 signed change is less
negative, indicating less subsequent improvement after regime exits. Rare-regime panels remain small.

The full-results default HTML export and alternate notebook controls (seed 4, layer 3, change since exit, regime 17,
cosine drift) were checked. The refreshed report compares all three arms; the original capped-only export is archived as
`outputs/m6_2_0/analysis_capped.html`.
